Most OT cyber incidents never penetrate the OT environment itself; attackers compromise IT systems and stop there because OT pivot requires specialized expertise, slows execution, and carries higher legal consequences than cybercrime. Modern factories have evolved to depend critically on IT systems—ERP, MES, shipping, inventory platforms—not direct PLC manipulation; when these systems are unavailable, production halts despite OT infrastructure remaining intact. The article challenges misconceptions that air gaps are sufficient protection and that security programs should optimize for nation-state threats rather than the financially-motivated cybercriminals that dominate industrial incidents. Sygnia proposes an anti-fragile resilience model: implementing local databases, cached data, and semi-manual procedures to maintain safe operations for 24–48 hours when IT is compromised, shifting focus from recovery to continuity.
