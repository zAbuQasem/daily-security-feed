The article argues that AI guardrails imposed by third-party LLM providers can undermine defenders' inherent advantage in security operations by blocking legitimate SOC investigations, creating delays that give attackers breathing room. The author advocates for 'operational sovereignty'—organizations maintaining full control over AI guardrail policies to customize or temporarily remove safeguards based on their threat model, rather than accepting inflexible provider-imposed restrictions. Cisco Talos evaluated 66 LLM and reasoning combinations for SOC use, finding that model selection requires balancing efficacy, speed, cost, and consistency; higher reasoning settings sometimes degrade performance despite expectations. The paper emphasizes that prompts, analyst personas, and model consistency drastically alter investigation outcomes, and recommends testing models against specific organizational workflows before deployment. The core insight is that operational sovereignty in agentic security systems requires internal control over AI guardrails to prevent adversarial interference with detection and response processes.
