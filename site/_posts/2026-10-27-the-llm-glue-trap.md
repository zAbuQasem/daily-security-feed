The article identifies a critical trade-off in LLM usage patterns across three interaction modes: line-autofill (high visibility), interactive chat (medium), and autonomous PR review (low visibility). As developers shift toward faster, less-visible modes, they sacrifice hands-on understanding and learning—paying for output they can't fully verify while losing the knowledge transfer that comes from authoring code. The deepest risk lies in fully autonomous LLM agents executing tasks unsupervised for hours or days with zero human oversight, creating supply-chain vulnerabilities through unreviewed code changes and developers who cannot recognize or catch deficiencies in generated output.
