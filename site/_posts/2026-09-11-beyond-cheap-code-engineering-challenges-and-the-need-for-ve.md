Sonar's podcast examines AI-assisted development and the critical need for verification as code generation becomes cheap. Research from Shaw and Nave (Wharton, 2026) shows people follow AI advice 79.8% of the time even when wrong—a phenomenon called 'cognitive surrender.' The article introduces the 'amplification thesis': AI enforces whatever standards you encode at machine scale, amplifying discipline or debt equally. Carnegie Mellon research found a temporary 3-5x velocity spike in the first month of AI adoption that reversed within three months, replaced by 41% complexity increase and 30% rise in issues. Key recommendation: place humans where judgment matters, use independent automated verification for volume, and maintain code quality as the input context that shapes future agent behavior.
