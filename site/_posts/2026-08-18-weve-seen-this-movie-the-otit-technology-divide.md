Manufacturing risks recreating its decades-old OT/IT organizational divide with AI adoption, as small language models move onto plant-floor edge devices and vision systems simultaneously with enterprise AI deployment, each driven and governed in isolation. Though CISA/NSA guidance (December 2025) explicitly calls for unified OT/IT leadership in AI risk management, organizational inertia and the default reflex to fold AI into existing siloed processes threaten to produce two separate programs: one in IT, one in OT, each with its own policy, approval process, and accountability framework. The result mirrors the historical IT/OT fragmentation that created persistent inventory, incident response, and security friction—this time with AI-influenced production decisions made without enterprise security visibility or coherent governance. Critically, AI adoption remains nascent (87% of manufacturers haven't meaningfully adopted), creating a narrow governance window to establish unified policy and accountability before habits harden. The cost is tangible: duplicated governance overhead, shadow AI risk from edge deployments, and unbudgeted liability when AI-driven decisions cause safety or quality incidents under no single accountable owner.
