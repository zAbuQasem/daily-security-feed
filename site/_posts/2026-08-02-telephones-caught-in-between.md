A technical history of AT&T's telephone leasing model and the infrastructure reasons behind it. The article explains why phones were always rented rather than sold: centralized network design made phones dependent on specific exchange configurations, party lines created cross-customer dependencies, compatibility required technician expertise, and a single malfunctioning phone could degrade service across an entire line. The practice reflects the fundamental architectural difference between centralized telephone networks (managed by the carrier) and distributed computer networks (interchangeable endpoints). This historical analysis provides context for the 1980s Bell System divestiture and the shift to customer premises equipment independence.
