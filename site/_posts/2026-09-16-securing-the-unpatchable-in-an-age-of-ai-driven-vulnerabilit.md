AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is accelerating the pace of finding exploitable bugs in legacy and unpatchable operational technology (OT) systems—medical devices, building management, critical infrastructure—that cannot be patched due to certification requirements or end-of-life status. The article argues air-gapped networks are impractical due to operational pressure for temporary connections, and instead recommends defense-in-depth through network visibility to identify vulnerable systems, micro-segmentation using VLANs and ACLs to restrict lateral movement, and next-generation firewalls with IPS to detect and block exploits at the network perimeter. Real-world examples include the 2017 WannaCry impact on NHS systems running Windows XP and 2023 exploitation of end-of-life software in governmental systems. This defensive architecture approach provides compensatory controls when patching is impossible, treating network architecture as the primary security boundary.
