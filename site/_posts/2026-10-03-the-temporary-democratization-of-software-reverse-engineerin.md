LLMs have democratized reverse engineering and CTF competition in 2026, with top Flare-On contestants now completing full challenges in hours using LLM-powered analysis rather than days of manual work. This shifts RE from a specialized human skill to a commoditized capability, raising concerns about the sustainability of competitive reverse engineering and the quality of security research when understanding becomes optional. The author (8th place Flare-On 2022) predicts an escalating arms race: vendors will deploy LLM-powered anti-RE defenses, software complexity will continue rising, and business logic will migrate server-side, ultimately creating a landscape where capability is dominated by computational resources rather than human expertise. The democratization provides significant accessibility gains but reduces signal-to-noise in research communities and creates barriers for those without substantial inference budgets.
