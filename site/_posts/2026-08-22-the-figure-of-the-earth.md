A detailed technical and historical account of geodesy—the precise measurement of Earth—centered on Brigadier Martin Hotine's Retriangulation of Great Britain (1935–1962). The article explains the foundational problem of determining Earth's true shape: while commonly approximated as a sphere, it must actually be modeled as an ellipsoid that accounts for rotational flattening and local gravity variations determined by crustal density. Triangulation and trilateration methods rely on reference ellipsoids anchored to specific datums, with measurements taken against this abstract surface rather than variable topography. The work established geodetic methods later applied to transcontinental and transoceanic surveying, with practical implications for aviation, mapping, and large-scale infrastructure projects.
