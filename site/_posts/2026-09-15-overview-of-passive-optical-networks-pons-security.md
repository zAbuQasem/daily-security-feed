Comprehensive technical analysis of security mechanisms in PON (Passive Optical Network) specifications used by ISPs for fiber-to-the-home connectivity. PONs broadcast shared optical signals to multiple subscribers on the same Optical Distribution Network, creating a vulnerability where unencrypted traffic metadata or full payloads are visible to co-subscribers—effectively exposing DNS queries, VoIP calls, and unencrypted protocols to all neighbors on the same ODN. The article reviews security features across five ITU-T specifications (GPON, XG-PON, XGS-PON, NG-PON2, 50G-PON), covering authentication schemes, key derivation mechanisms, and encryption methods used to isolate subscriber traffic. Highly relevant to infrastructure security, ISP operations, and the millions of European broadband users relying on these networks.
