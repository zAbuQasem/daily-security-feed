A technical deep-dive into Bell Labs' T-carrier digital telephone system (T1, T2, T3), explaining how the digital hierarchy emerged from backwards-compatible channel bank design rather than top-down architecture. The D-series channel banks (D1, D2, D3, D4) used techniques like 7.833-bit PCM samples and robbed-bit signaling to increase capacity from 24 to 96 to thousands of channels while maintaining compatibility with existing T1 infrastructure. The article traces the evolution from SIGSALY (WWII encrypted voice system) through early PCM to the practical engineering compromises that shaped modern digital telecommunications infrastructure, offering infrastructure security professionals historical context for understanding legacy telecom systems and their technical constraints.
